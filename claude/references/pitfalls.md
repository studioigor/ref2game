# Pitfalls and speed rules

Each entry is a class of defect that cost real time: what it looks like, why it happens, the principle of the fix, and how to catch it. The fix is a principle, not the only way; find the best one for this project.

## Where the time goes, and how not to lose it

| Slow | Fast |
|---|---|
| Studying for an hour before anything is visible | Time-box the study; **the template runs on placeholders in minute one**, and real art lands piece by piece soon after. |
| Generating one asset, waiting, then the next | Launch generation jobs **in parallel** (`gen.py batch --jobs N`). Wire code and effects while they run. |
| Painting the whole scene as one image | One depth band or piece per call. Effects and motion in code. The level as data. |
| Generating animation frames | Part sheets + rigs + procedural motion (`animation.md`) |
| Judging from memory or full frames | Contact sheets, magnified crops, cycle sheets (`sheet.py`); critics measure |
| Authors checking their own work | Independent critics per asset batch, module, motion, frame and game, running in the background (`critics.md`) |
| Endless critique loops | At most 3 rounds; leftovers become "known differences" |
| Re-rendering videos for every check | Stills at the affected beats; the live page is the deliverable; video only on request |
| Hand-fixing derived files | Fix sources (`art/gen`, code). `prep.py` is a pure function of `art/gen`. |
| Tuning the demo route by eye | `tune.cjs`; search timings headless in node; put pickups on the computed arcs |

## Bugs that cost hours before

**Generation and assets**
- **A CLI generator ran without the prompt.** The tool reads its prompt from a channel it wasn't given (stdin vs an argument). Pass it the way that tool reads it and confirm with a probe generation whose prompt is unmistakable; `gen.py`'s codex provider pipes it through stdin with `-- -`.
- **Halos and fringes.** The key colour or a dark matte survives in the edge pixels. Un-mix the edges, bleed RGB outward under transparent pixels, upload premultiplied, and choose a key colour far from the asset's palette. *Detect:* composite on light and dark backgrounds; the alpha-fringe count (`critics.md` §7).
- **A state variant pops on swap** (colour, position or scale jump). It was generated separately and never aligned or colour-matched to its base. Align and match it (`variantfix.py`) before it reaches the page. *Detect:* flip between base and variant in place, magnified.
- **A fix vanished after the next rebuild.** It was written to a derived file (`live/assets`) that `prep.py` regenerates from `art/gen`. Fix sources only.
- **A sheet slices into the wrong number of pieces.** Parts touch or detached bits split off. Ask the generator for wide empty gaps, adjust `slice.py --dilate`, and list the components before slicing. *Detect:* compare the component count with the expected list.
- **A cut edge shows.** A piece cropped by its canvas (the top of a hanging element) is placed where its cut edge is visible and seems to float. Anchor cut edges inside an occluder and draw them behind it. *Detect:* stills at both camera extremes.

**Rendering**
- **WebGL can't load images from file://.** Always serve over http (`serve.sh`, or the tools' built-in servers). If a port is taken, pick the next free one.
- **A custom draw path skips state the shared path sets.** A sprite drawn outside `L.sprite()` without its sub-rect uniform (`u_src`) shows the wrong region of the atlas (a whole part squeezed into another's rect). Go through the library, or set every uniform it sets.
- **Hairlines or black specks at quad edges.** A shader function got an argument outside its domain (`pow` of a negative base, a divide by zero, `sqrt` of a negative) and produced NaN, usually on padded edges. Clamp inputs to the valid domain. *Detect:* magnified stills; bisect layers with `?only=`.
- **A faint box around atlas sprites at minified scales.** Texture sampling inside a branch or after an early return leaves the mip derivatives undefined at the quad's border, so a coarse mip is sampled and neighbouring atlas sprites bleed in. Derivatives must come from uniform control flow: compute them (or sample) before any branch, then mask. Padding alone never fixes it. *Detect:* magnified stills on a contrasting background at every zoom the game uses.
- **Layer edges exposed at camera extremes.** A layer that moves at a different rate than the camera (especially foreground faster than the play plane), or framing art narrower than the camera's travel, runs out of picture. Make every layer cover the full camera range (wider art, wrapped or mirrored continuation, or a clamped camera). *Detect:* stills at both extremes of every camera axis.
- **Glows wash out actors.** Additive light over actors and bright skies erases their contrast. Limit glow strength where actors stand, and give small bright things a backing against bright backgrounds. *Detect:* the squint test and actor-vs-surroundings contrast (`critics.md` §7).
- **A time-driven effect froze in play** (rain hanging in the air, embers that never flicker) while every still looked right. Shader uniforms belong to their program and keep their last value; the helper never passed the time, so the effect rendered at time 0 forever. Every animated helper takes the time and passes it on every draw. *Detect:* frames 1/60 s apart with and without the effect (`qa.mjs` anim).
- **Something swept or strobed while a state changed** (fog racing across the screen as the weather came in). A phase computed as rate(state) × absolute time jumps by the change in rate × t, and t is large late in a session. Integrate phases over time in the sim, or keep the rate fixed and let the state change amounts only. *Detect:* frames 1/60 s apart during the change vs at steady state, at a late world time (`qa.mjs` ramps).
- **Flicker between renders.** `Math.random`, `Date` or accumulated state in the scene. Every frame must be a pure function of t and sim state. *Detect:* render the same t twice and diff (`compare.py diff`).
- **One global light multiply for ground and actors.** A strong tinted ambient shifts skin and whites into the tint, and local lights bright enough to colour the ground blow out the characters. Ground and actors need to take light differently: build darkness mostly from the ground's own values with a mild ambient, separate ground from actors in the lighting pass, and give actors a reduced, less saturated share of local light so they keep their colours. *Detect:* sample skin and whites under the scene's light vs the reference (`compare.py tones`).

**Code**
- **TDZ crash: "Cannot access 'x' before initialization".** A helper is used before its `const`/`let` declaration has run. Hoist helpers to module scope. *Detect:* `livecheck.mjs` reports page errors.
- **Duplicated or lost code after a scripted edit.** The edit sliced a file between two search indices without checking their order or uniqueness. Assert positions and match counts when editing by script, and syntax-check afterwards (`node -e "new Function(src)"` for scene files, `node --check` for modules).
- **Coupled changes landed separately.** Textures changed resolution before the code that sizes them, and the hot-reloading page showed giant sprites for minutes. Land an asset and the code that depends on it in one step.
- **A render error in one rarely reached state froze the game loop.** The loop logs a render error once and keeps running, and `SCENE.QA().states` covers every action × every facing, so `qa.mjs` reports the throw.

**Simulation and timing**
- **A feel change broke the scripted demo.** Hit-stop freezes the hero's clock; a reaction that edits the player's velocity changes the jump; every later demo event shifts. Reactions move what reacts, not the player, unless that is the intent. *Detect:* rerun `tune.cjs` after every feel or physics change.
- **Respawned pickups were collected twice or never came back.** One flag can't tell "available", "taken this life" and "banked from an earlier life" apart. Give each state its own explicit value; only "available" is collectable. *Detect:* a QA state or `tune.cjs` run across a death and respawn.
- **An enemy never attacked.** Props were moved after the spawns were laid out, and one collider landed on a spawn: the actor started inside a blocked cell, and every step it tried was refused, so it stood in "chase" forever. Positions produced by separate placement passes must be validated together, after the last pass: spawns move to the nearest open space, and movement lets an actor that starts inside a blocked cell step out. *Detect:* `SIM.selfCheck()` in `tune.cjs` (every spawn open; every enemy, met by an idle player, reaches him and attacks).
- **Reactions popped at the attract loop seam.** Something triggered by the demo (a critter in flight, a late gust) was still running when the loop reset. Everything the demo triggers settles inside the loop. *Detect:* stills just before and just after the loop point.

**Polish (defects that reached the user before a check did; each now has a rule or a check)**
- **The wrong rendering family.** The whole frame, type included, rendered at a low-res target for a reference whose type and light are smooth, and it read as "too pixelated" at once. Decide the rendering family by measuring same-scale crops (`styles.md` §1; polish checklist item 1).
- **Variable text overflowed its panel.** The panel had a fixed size. Size panels to their content (`L.labelW`), register them (`L.uiBox`), and put worst-case strings into `SCENE.QA().states`; `qa.mjs` fails on overflow.
- **Overlays anchored to one actor overlapped** (several labels and a talk bubble). Stack overlays that share an anchor and give the space around the actor to one owner at a time. *Detect:* stage the worst case (all of them at once) as a QA state; `qa.mjs` fails overlapping text.
- **Emitted effects started from the wrong place** (the actor's ground point, its back, the hip). The sim's origin and the drawn holder were computed separately, and an emitter has several layers (visible haze, its contribution to the light map, a glow, the held item) that each computed their own; fixing one left the others wrong. One origin function, shared by the sim and the rig, feeds every layer; when facing changes where the item is held, the item and the origin move together. *Detect:* `L.anchor` and the `qa.mjs` anchors check in every facing, at each facing's extreme aim angles, not only straight ahead.
- **Effects and light ignored occlusion.** Overlay passes (glows, haze, beams) don't take part in the depth sort, so flames covered an actor standing in front of them and a beam emitted while facing away appeared to start on the actor's back. Anything with a ground position goes into the depth sort; layers that stay in overlay passes are moved into the sorted band (or hidden) when their emitter is behind an occluder. An actor never receives its own emitted light: cut its silhouette out of that light. *Detect:* stills with actors in front of and behind every emitter, in every facing.
- **Primitive-shape effects** (ground cracks as straight dark strips from the feet, flat rings, plain bars) read as programmer art, even with dust on top. Effects need shape, layers and a life: an origin at the real contact point, a form that matches the event, parts that grow, cool and fade at different rates, occlusion by sprites, and a trace left behind (`effects.md` §4; `L.cracks` exists for ground impacts). *Detect:* polish checklist item 11, an age strip, magnified.
- **A tiling piece with end details doubled them at every joint.** Tile from a `src` crop without the ends and close each run with one end piece. *Detect:* joints, magnified.
- **Lights sized from a gameplay radius flooded and merged** into one mass. A visual derived from a gameplay number rarely matches the reference. Light shapes take their size, edge and colour from the reference's lights; show gameplay ranges separately when needed. *Detect:* the fidelity critic's light row (`critics.md` §5a).
- **A contact shadow didn't match its footprint** (an ellipse under a long rectangular base reads as a floating disc). Shape the shadow from the footprint measured in the sprite's alpha, offset away from the light. *Detect:* magnified crops; polish checklist item 7.
- **Code-drawn add-ons didn't match the art** (a code-drawn body part on a painted character) and were rejected. Match the rendering exactly (outline, shading, texture density), or cut them. *Detect:* polish checklist items 1–3.

**Rigs and motion**
- **Fake animation.** Only the player was animated; NPCs "worked" by tilting the whole sprite, a worker turned away from their station to face the player, animals only swayed, nobody walked, and squash pulses alone read as no animation at all. Every actor in the animation list gets a rig with real joints (`animation.md` §12–13): articulated limbs, follow-through, workers facing their station, some NPCs walking routines. *Detect:* `qa.mjs` fails rigid-only motion and listed actors without a loop; the motion critic.
- **Checks too weak to see rig defects.** A limp, feet in the ground, a weapon pointing away at the hit and popping deaths passed a glance at stills plus one limb-motion number. Rigs report their joints and `qa.mjs` measures them on every change (`animation.md` §14); every defect a viewer finds that the numbers missed becomes a new check.
- **Walkers glided or only bobbed, limb parts didn't match the approved drawing, a held item sat on the wrong layer.** `animation.md` §11–12. *Detect:* the `qa.mjs` motion check (aligned on the upper body, so a bob can't fake it) and the motion critic on the cycle sheets.
- **An unriggable source drawing.** A walker generated mid-stride and split at the hips can only translate its halves: legs slide over each other and clothing travels with them. Generate actors in a rig pose (limbs separated, clear gaps between legs, hems and sleeves clear of the joints) and in the views the movement needs (a turnaround, so moving away shows the back). Measure joints from the alpha, never hand-tune per sprite.
- **Bends in an axis the view doesn't show.** In front and back views a knee bent sideways by a picture-plane solver reads as bow legs or broken shins; the real bend points at the camera. Show it the way the view shows it (swing about the hip, foreshorten the lower limb); picture-plane knee bends belong to views that show the bend. *Detect:* cycle sheets per view.
- **Screen depth mistaken for limb length.** In an iso view a foot stepping toward the camera sits lower on screen; a picture-plane leg solver reads that as "leg too short" and drops the hips at every double-support frame. Separate a foot target's travel and lift (for the solver) from its screen depth (for the projection). *Detect:* the hip trace over a cycle; the `qa.mjs` rig checks; cycle sheets frame by frame.
- **3/4 rigs read as unnatural even with planted feet** (crouching strides, stubby trailing thighs, arms bent out at rest, a far arm sticking out, a tool held edge-on, a weapon on the chest while facing away, gaps at elbows). Limbs move in 3D and are projected; held items follow the projected forearm and go behind the body when it faces away; joints hide their seams (`animation.md` §15.1–15.5).
- **Fixed part order across views and depths.** One draw order put the far weapon arm over both legs in the back view, and a many-limbed creature's near limbs behind its body. Order parts per view and by depth: far parts before the body, near parts after; which side is near swaps between front and back views. A rule that moves a part across the body (a far arm reaching forward comes in front of the chest) holds only in the views it was written for: forward points toward the camera in front views and away from it in back views, where the same reach goes behind the back. *Detect:* the motion critic's layering check on every view's cycle sheet, with reaches and work poses staged in back views too (the far limb moving).
- **Standing actors stood at attention, legs crossed from behind.** The idle pose was the painted part layout. Standing gets a stance of its own (feet wider than the hips, weight on one leg, slow weight shifts); the `qa.mjs` stance check fails the bare layout (`animation.md` §15.4). A QA reference pose that should be the painted layout says so (`neutral`), or soft knees fail the stretch check.
- **The hit landed while the weapon pointed away from the target.** A swing that reversed back through the rest pose put the damage frame at the top of the windup. Make an attack one arc that keeps turning the same way toward an end aim computed from the target's direction, put the damage frame at the end of the strike, and draw the trail from the real tip path. *Detect:* the `qa.mjs` aim-at-impact check on attacks staged against targets all round.
- **Pops on interrupts, deaths and stops.** A death in one attack phase snapped the arm to rest, swapping the rig for one body sprite dropped limbs in a frame, and zeroing the gait amount on stop snapped the feet. Every phase of an action carries through an interrupt or death and blends out; the rig stays whole through a collapse before crossfading to remains; gait amounts are smoothed and keep the last travel direction. *Detect:* the `qa.mjs` pops check on transitions staged in `SCENE.QA()`; transition sheets.
- **A soft-bone warp drew holes or tore.** The inverse map found the background pixel under the moved part (it maps to itself with zero error) and kept it; a weight ramp over a whole region curled it, and a weight cut off at a region's edge tore a notch. Among exact candidates prefer the opaque one, then the one that moved more; bend at the base and fade weights at the seam (`animation.md` §15.6). *Detect:* magnified frames at the pose extremes.

**Process**
- **The world felt like a static picture** even with many loops. Loops alone don't make a world alive. Add shared causes (wind), player-triggered reactions, camera travel and traces (`gamefeel.md`). *Detect:* the motion-energy map (`critics.md` §7); the game critic's aliveness score.
- **Guessing the user's intent.** A wrong guess about the game, the player or the scope costs a rebuild. Ask with short options; decide technical matters yourself.
- **Critics ran only after the user asked.** They launch by default at the gates, in the background, without slowing the loop (`critics.md` §0, §9).

## Definition of done (Gate B and final delivery)

- [ ] `qa.mjs` passes (every HUD state at worst-case strings, every emitter anchor, every walk cycle, and the measured rig checks for every actor in the animation list: foot slide, ground, lift, limp, stride, knee, stretch, stance, arm phase, rigid-only motion, aim at the impact, pops, coverage; every time-driven effect moving and every state change free of sweeps).
- [ ] The polish checklist (`critics.md` §0) passed in the frame and game critics.
- [ ] The live page loads with 0 errors and holds 60 fps (`livecheck.mjs` fails below `--minfps`, default 55); hot reload keeps play state.
- [ ] `tune.cjs` passes: the attract demo reaches its beats and shows the key interactions.
- [ ] Every asset passed its asset critic; every module passed its module critic (≤ 3 rounds, leftovers listed).
- [ ] The frame, fidelity and game critics ran on the current build; scores are reported.
- [ ] The interaction audit (`gamefeel.md` §7) is ticked for what this game has.
- [ ] The hand-back message gives the live URL, what to try in play mode (keys, places, reactions), the critic scores, known differences, and the next steps.
